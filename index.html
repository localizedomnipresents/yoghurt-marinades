Six mild marinades

Yogurt-based, no chilli in any of them. Nothing here goes hotter than a grind of black pepper, so the whole table eats the same dinner.

250 g plain yogurt to every kilo of chicken.

The same five moves every time

All six work identically, so this bit only needs reading once.

  • Mix the marinade in a bowl before the chicken goes anywhere near it. Dry spices need a moment in the yogurt to stop tasting powdery.
  • Thighs over breasts. Cheaper, more forgiving, better flavour. Breasts work but pull them out at 12 hours or the texture goes soft.
  • Fridge, 4 to 24 hours. Overnight is the sweet spot. Past a day the yogurt stops improving anything.
  • Scrape most of it off before cooking. A thin film browns. A thick coat steams, then burns.
  • Medium heat to 74 °C inside. Yogurt and sugar catch fast, so resist a hot pan or a hot grill.

Built without Jamaican curry powder

Jamaican blends are hard to find locally, so this rebuilds one: a mild supermarket curry powder as the base, with extra turmeric and allspice to push it in the right direction. The tablespoon of fresh ginger covers the ground ginger a true Jamaican blend would bring, so there is none listed separately.

Skip Durban masala and anything labelled hot or extra hot — both are chilli-forward and will overshoot by a long way.

Built without annatto

Annatto barely registers as flavour — its job in a pibil marinade is that red-orange stain plus a faint earthiness. Sweet paprika, a little turmeric and a teaspoon of tomato paste do the same work with things you already own.

If you do track down the real thing at a spice merchant, ask for annatto seeds or achiote and use two teaspoons ground in place of those three lines. Avoid achiote pastes, which usually have chilli blended in.
